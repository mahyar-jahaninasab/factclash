## 1. Context

Passing extensive raw context blocks directly into an LLM prompt leads to high token consumption, attention dilution, and non-deterministic hallucinations.

Furthermore, to take reliable downstream actions, the system must operate on verified **premises**.

## 2. Decision Drivers

* **Context Window Optimization:** Drastically reduce token payload sent to downstream engines by stripping out irrelevant text and feeding only validated, non-contradictory premise-hypothesis units.
* **Deterministic Fact-Clash Detection:** Systematically identify conflicting premises prior to execution without relying on opaque LLM generative steps.

* **Latency and Resource Efficiency:** Execute premise identification and entailment scoring locally.

* **Directional Logical Calibration:** Ensure that context validation measures true directional inference.



## 3. Decision

We decide to implement a non-LLM **Hybrid Premise-Hypothesis Resolution Architecture** composed of targeted sub-methods operating in a multi-stage execution pipeline.


#### Method 1: Symbolic & Natural Logic / Syntactic Parsing (e.g., NatLog, Dependency Tree Alignment)

* **Mechanism:** Converts surface text into syntactic dependency trees and projects lexical relations (e.g., hypernymy, negation, intersective modifiers) upward through composition trees using monotonicity calculus.

* **Pros:** 100% interpretable; yields strict mathematical proofs; zero risk of hallucination; negligible computational footprint.

* **Cons:** Extremely low recall on open-domain unstructured text; fails when syntax deviates or complex paraphrasing occurs.

* **When Useful:** Rapid deterministic validation of crisp structural rules, numerical bounds, and downward/upward monotone statements (e.g., legal or compliance constraints).



#### Method 2: Bi-Encoder Dense Retrieval & Structural Chunking (e.g., Sentence-Transformers)

* **Mechanism:** Projects sentence spans into dense vector spaces independently ($E(P)$ and $E(H)$) and calculates cosine similarity or vector distance for candidate pair extraction.
* **Pros:** High processing speed ($O(N)$ vector indexing); scales to massive document corpora; effectively clusters related candidate premise groups.
* **Cons:** Measures symmetric *semantic similarity* rather than directional *logical entailment*; easily fooled by high lexical overlap between contradictory statements.

* **When Useful:** Stage-1 candidate retrieval to isolate potential premise-hypothesis pairs from bloated context blocks before deep logical verification.

#### Method 3: Pre-trained Cross-Encoder Classifiers (e.g., DeBERTa-v3 Cross-Attention)

* **Mechanism:** Concatenates premise and hypothesis sequences (`[CLS] Premise [SEP] Hypothesis [SEP]`) into deep bi-directional cross-attention layers, outputting exact 3-way class probabilities: $P(\text{Entailment})$, $P(\text{Neutral})$, and $P(\text{Contradiction})$.


* **Pros:** High directional reasoning accuracy; robust against paraphrase shifts; robust detection of subtle fact-inversions and negations.


* **Cons:** Higher computational complexity ($O(N \cdot M)$ pair comparisons); sequence lengths constrained (typically max 512 tokens per pair).


* **When Useful:** Pairwise directional entailment scoring, assigning numerical support confidence, and constructing cross-premise contradiction matrices.



#### Method 4: Ensemble Debiasing & Hypothesis-Only Filtering (e.g., Product of Experts - PoE)

* **Mechanism:** Integrates a secondary hypothesis-only model during feature extraction to penalize predictions that rely on surface-level dataset artifacts or giveaway keywords (such as isolated negation terms).


* **Pros:** Prevents models from guessing entailment/contradiction without truly processing the premise.


* **Cons:** Requires lightweight fine-tuning of the cross-encoder pipeline.
* **When Useful:** Ensuring high classification reliability in noisy real-world data containing repetitive negation patterns.

## 4. Consequences

### 4.1 Pros

* **Effective Context De-bloating:** Drops prompt context size, preserving only premises that hold explicit directional entailment with operational hypotheses.
* **Deterministic Fact-Clash Identification:** Resolves premise conflicts before execution, eliminating AI failures caused by feeding contradictory context into generative engines.
* **High Operational Latency & Cost Efficiency:** Replaces costly, high-latency multi-thousand-token LLM inference passes with fast, local GPU/CPU batch processing over cross-encoders.

* **Debiased Reliability:** Combining cross-attention encoders with debiased filtering eliminates reliance on surface lexical shortcuts and hypothesis-only artifacts.



### 4.2 Cons

* **Architectural Complexity:** Requires deploying and managing a multi-stage pipeline (bi-encoder retriever + symbolic parser + cross-encoder runtime) alongside standard application stacks.
* **Pairwise Matrix Overhead ($O(N \cdot M)$):** If Stage 1 bi-encoder filtering is too permissive, running dense cross-encoders on large candidate pair combinations can introduce processing latency.
* **Domain Adaptation Requirements:** Cross-encoder models pre-trained on generic NLI corpora (e.g., MNLI, FEVER) may require domain-specific fine-tuning if premise texts contain specialized legal, medical, or technical jargon.
